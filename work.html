Otter Rock: Beneath the Surface

Client: Oregon State Parks Role: Design Contributor & Comm. Strategist Timeframe: 2025–2026 Channels: Snap AR, Spatial Computing Metrics: 2026 Webby Honoree

The Challenge

Environmental education at Oregon State Parks struggled to convey the unseen complexity of marine ecosystems using traditional static signage. We needed to bridge this gap and make invisible marine life tangible to everyday visitors without funding expensive physical installations.

Audience & Insight

We targeted casual park visitors and families. The key insight: people don't want to read dense placards—they want to explore. If we could anchor spatial narratives to existing physical park signage, we could turn passive reading into active, memorable discovery.

Strategy

As part of the Snap AR scholarship, I pitched an augmented reality strategy to Oregon State Parks. By leveraging spatial computing, we built an interactive educational layer over the physical environment, using visitors' own devices to lower the barrier to entry.

Execution

I contributed to the concept, UX design, and communications strategy. We built an interactive AR lens that allowed visitors to explore subterranean tidal habitats and kelp forests in real-time simply by scanning a park sign.

Results

The project successfully transformed static park infrastructure into dynamic educational touchpoints. Oregon State Parks adopted the lens, proving that emerging spatial computing technologies can drive tangible engagement in public-interest conservation efforts, and the project was honored at the 2026 Webby Awards.

Reflection

Prototyping in AR taught me that the best technology gets out of the way. If I were to do this again, I’d focus even earlier on onboarding UX for older park visitors who aren't digital natives.

ARHR: Visualizing Reproductive Health

The Alliance for Reproductive Health Rights (ARHR) had conducted rigorous research into free sanitary pad policy advocacy and gender equality across Ghana. The structural problem was translation: highly academic research needed to be transformed into accessible visual stories that could mobilize policy makers and community audiences alike without losing nuance.

I traveled to rural communities to document the reality on the ground through photography and on-camera interviews. Operating at the intersection of journalism and advocacy, I executed a comprehensive communications audit and designed a suite of shareable infographics that effectively communicated culturally sensitive research to international donors.

By synthesizing field data into donor-aligned narrative frameworks, I directly supported the drafting of grant proposals that drove policy engagement. The project reinforced a core strategic truth: global communication requires deep cultural empathy. The most effective narratives center the dignity of the community while aggressively advancing organizational goals.

AARP Oregon: Mobilizing the 50+ Community Through Data-Informed Narrative

Voter turnout among older adults is historically high, but deeply engaging them on specific local policy initiatives requires moving beyond mass broadcasting. AARP Oregon needed a targeted, localized narrative strategy to drive active legislative lobbying for the state's most pressing civic issues.

As the creative lead for the Voter Engagement Series, I architected a multi-channel advocacy framework. I produced and launched a targeted podcast series designed to demystify local politics, translating complex legislative jargon into clear, actionable community impact stories. I also drafted high-leverage press releases that secured placements across six major regional publications.

The integration of audio storytelling with traditional PR dramatically increased civic participation. By centering the voices of local advocates and pairing them with rigorous policy data, the campaign didn't just inform voters—it mobilized them, resulting in a 44% lift in click-through rates on digital advocacy channels.

Homeless in Humboldt: A Three-Part Docuseries

In local advocacy, human experiences are often reduced to abstract statistics. I led the creative direction and production of a multi-episode documentary series to bridge this disconnect, highlighting the lived reality of people experiencing homelessness in Northern California through long-form, human-centered video journalism.

Over a longitudinal reporting journey, my team and I shadowed JP Urich. We documented daily survival, community encampment sweeps, and his eventual path to stable housing. Managing all facets of field production, cinematography, and editing required earning deep trust and maintaining uncompromised ethical storytelling standards.

The docuseries was syndicated regionally across local media outlets, elevating empathetic dialogue around community housing solutions. Deep listening and narrative integrity are the foundations of compelling communication—this project reinforced that the ability to tell authentic human stories shapes everything I do as a strategist.
